264	The battles for the Antares system were fought with varying success for eight years. It was a strategically important system, the intersection point of most trade and transport routes, and the Coalition intended to stand to the end in its attempts first to keep the dominators out of its borders, then to prevent them from landing on uninhabited planets with deposits of natural resources that would significantly strengthen the might of their ships. When these attempts failed, and the system nevertheless passed to the blazeroids, every year the military and rangers continued to attempt to win Antares back, and each time the dominator fleet proved stronger. The outskirts of the system were filled with tons of space debris: half-empty fuel tanks, fragments of antennas, radars and engines torn apart by powerful explosions, a fine crumble of broken glass sparkling in the light of distant stars, transmitters blinking with tiny lights, all these years broadcasting an SOS signal on all frequencies... Years later this ring of space debris would turn into a historical monument. Everything would be left as is, and a plaque installed on one of the asteroids would preserve the names of all the ships that found their end here.
265	In December of 3314 it all ended. In maloq society suicidal acts committed for the sake of the Great Cause were encouraged, and Ordan fired the first shot at Blazer. 269	Khaynakin rarely exited hyperspace on manual control - he was used to the computer itself determining the best exit point, free of ships, asteroids and other space bodies. But today he had to hide his appearance from hundreds of dominator combat units, and most importantly - from Terron, who at the moment possessed nearly the most powerful and perfect radar of all those existing in the Galaxy. The ranger had never had a scanner, he had switched off the gripper and the droid as unnecessary, and had even removed the shield generator from its slot, cutting all the cables - back on the approach to the borders of the Shomma system. The computer was the last to lose power - Faylwoker set the coordinates for a hyperjump - not far at all, just eight parsecs - waited until the countdown to launch began on the engine panel, and then ordered the computer to shut down, ignoring all standard warnings. And so - the hyperflight lasted mere seconds, though in fact a couple of tens of minutes passed - and Khaynakin barely managed to grab the right levers. The braking turned out sharp, and the ranger would have slammed his face into the cockpit glass if not for the seat belts that bit painfully into his skin. The radar was off along with all the other equipment, but today the Earthman did not need to know the distance to the planets and the number of ships in the system. Today Khaynakin Faylwoker needed to press just one button - that was all that was required of him.<br>It seemed he had exited hyperspace too far from the star - the cockpit was plunged into darkness, and only a single bulb blinked yellow on the control panel, the only device now using the ship's batteries. In the distance a star blazed with bright light, and around it a green-gleaming steel sphere lazily wound circles - and for several minutes Khaynakin could not tear his eyes from Terron, watching spellbound every glint the light of the central star cast off the metal. His retinue's ships were not visible from such a distance - but by the time they could catch the first signal from the ranger's ship, it would be too late. Perhaps Khaynakin would manage to get away. 270	At last the ranger came out of his momentary stupor.
