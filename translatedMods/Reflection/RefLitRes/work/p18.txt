173	The Rangers' Center station lazily rotated on its axis in Mars orbit, as if it had deliberately maneuvered closer to Earth, awaiting the arrival of a guest. Space on this day was surprisingly empty in general - there was none of the usual crowd of transport ships waiting for landing clearance, nor any pirates entertaining the waiting ships with interesting offers. Strangely, there were not even any military ships, although those were infrequent guests in the Sun system anyway - one of the most peaceful systems of this arm of the galaxy. Rare asteroids pierced space at unimaginable speeds, rounded the Sun and rushed back to the Asteroid Belt or even farther - to where not every pilot would risk sticking his nose without a decent hull and sturdy armor.<br>The only explanation the pilot of the lone ship could find for this lull was the dominator attack on the Antares system. It had recently been seized by that hostile race, and the military had quickly drawn up a plan to liberate the system: any free ship could join the operation, be it ranger, trader, liner or pirate. Naturally, there was a category of people endowed with common sense and a self-preservation instinct - of course, no one would dare brand them traitors or deserters - it was just that these people knew their wretched ship and industrial laser were good for nothing but trading minerals, and certainly not for space battles. Glory, awards, trophy micromodules and rating points - all this occupies an important place in the life of any ranger, but not so important as to lose one's life in its prime. At most, a couple of medals on the gravestone would immortalize him in the memory of descendants.
174	- That's good then! - Faylyaney declared, ticking a box on some form, and explained: - The government of the planet Khalgalla has contacted us and asked us to send them a person for a rather... specific assignment. They specifically needed a ranger, as a person with steeled nerves and an iron will... - glancing doubtfully at Khaynakin, the faeyan continued: - We analyzed the data on all registered rangers and came to the conclusion that you suit us!<br>- I suit, uh... for what? - Khaynakin did not understand. Only one thing got through to him - nobody here intended to bully him morally. At least, not yet.<br>- For the assignment from the government of the planet Khalgalla, - Faylyaney repeated patiently. - They sent a list of requirements, - the faeyan showed the ranger that same form, studded with ticks in every item except the last. - It is desirable to arrive on the planet before the eighteenth of August, and there they will explain what your task is. Therefore, you have a week to think it over...<br>- Wait, - Khaynakin said anxiously, - I won't know in advance what I'll have to do? That's not what we agreed...
175	- We haven't agreed on anything yet, - Faylyaney interrupted the barely begun tirade. - But recall the contract you signed on this very spot five years ago - you obligated yourself to serve the good of the Galaxy and apply every effort for its prosperity, did you not? Consider this an assignment from the Galaxy Council, for which the client planet will pay you quite well. Naturally, you may refuse. But you mentioned financial difficulties, and your background is not as clean as you would like.<br>It was a dead end - the faeyan was unmistakably hinting that the assignment was important, since it required a person versed in relations between races, laws and rules of interracial etiquette, but no specifics - who knew what the planet Khalgalla wanted from him. On the other hand, Khaynakin was already trying the Galaxy Council's patience with his inactivity, and the money would be very welcome. Space rangers were mercenaries, those who voluntarily took on a task beyond most people in this war - they could often afford to do absolutely anything, but sooner or later each of them received an assignment he had no right to refuse. Faylyaney seemed good-natured and relaxed, but his gaze eloquently reminded Khaynakin who he had been before he signed that ill-fated contract. A document that had provided him with forgiveness, freedom and a chance to start everything anew, at a price that had not seemed so high then.
176	- So, the eighteenth of August, - Khaynakin repeated, trying to stay calm. He did not want to go back to prison. But he could not shake the thought that the war-exhausted Galaxy Council could promise a high reward only to those who might not return. Just as it had not bothered him for a long five years, which meant there must have been some reason for it.<br>Faylyaney brightened.<br>- I had no doubt I could rely on you, - he said. - Depart immediately. In the next hall Laymak will issue you a diplomatic pass and make changes to the onboard computer of your ship - you will fly with the cloaking device of a diplomatic vessel. From this minute forget your true status, try not to make contact with unknown ships and not to bicker with pirates - no one must know the true reason for your visit to Khalgalla. You will be met at the spaceport.
177	In the vicinity of Khalgalla no problems arose - the system was full of military ships clearly planning some operation, the pirates had hidden to be safe and kept their noses out of sight. Faylwoker was about to land on the planet in the commotion, but a pair of warships blocked his way. The ranger grew alarmed - Laymak had assured him that from the outside the ship looked like a real diplomatic liner of human make, and the pass was genuine, certified according to all the rules. And to verify this, the military had only to scan the ship once, whose onboard computer would instantly give out all the necessary information.<br>- Mr. Trikli? This is Fighter Kharmin speaking, - came from the speakers. Khaynakin at first did not understand what had prompted the soldier to address him so mysteriously, and then remembered that Valley Trikli was his name on the diplomatic pass. - State your destination planet and purpose of visit...<br>- Valley Trikli here, - Khaynakin responded. It was strange that the military had taken an interest in his modest ship - it clearly did not look like a pirate and certainly broke no rules of interstellar flight. - Destination is the planet Khalgalla, purpose of visit is conducting negotiations, I represent the planet Earth in the Sun system.
178	- I must contact the planetary government and inquire about your landing permission, - Kharmin said officially and signed off. A few minutes later the speaker came alive again: - Your permission is confirmed, - said Kharmin, back on the line. - The government of Khalgalla welcomes you to our system and awaits your prompt landing. I apologize for such obstacles, however, these are the instructions of the military base "Vintar". Attacks on peaceful ships by pirates with forged documents and hulls have become more frequent in the system. Have a good flight.<br>Both warships circled Faylwoker a bit longer and went away. A few moments later a rumble sounded - the military had slightly deviated from their direct duties and shot up a couple of asteroids. Eh, if it didn't look so strange, one could have tried to pick up the minerals and sell them where it paid best - you wouldn't make much money that way, but it's never superfluous. Sighing in parting, Khaynakin gave the computer the landing command. No longer listening to the monotonous "Command accepted, distance: 318, estimated landing time...", the ranger went to the porthole and tried to distract himself from all sorts of thoughts with the marvelous space scenery.
179	The Khalgalla spaceport was packed with ships to capacity - mostly passenger liners and transport vessels, but here and there typical pirate ships and military fighters could be seen. The pilots did not seem bothered by such proximity, as if the conflict of their interests took place exclusively in space. Keeping his surprise to himself, Faylwoker climbed out of the ship and looked around for the promised greeters. Instead of a government representative, three tall fellows in the blue uniforms of the human Armed Forces were already hurrying toward the ranger.<br>- Identification! - one of them barked.<br>- Trade license! - the second demanded.<br>- Ship documents, quick! And we need to check the contents of your hold, - the third declared and was already heading to the ship, intending to climb inside, when a sharp shout stopped him.<br>- Captain Fenton! - came from behind - a balding middle-aged man had already gotten out of a very stately-looking car, in a strict business suit and with a completely impenetrable face. - Mr. Trikli is a diplomat, he does not engage in trade, and you certainly won't find contraband goods in his hold. I suggest we leave him alone and part amicably...<br>- We apologize, Mr. Trikli, - the third soldier said at once - apparently the highest in rank. - And you, Mr. Ambassador.
180	Exchanging embarrassed glances, the soldiers withdrew, leaving only the puzzled Khaynakin and the amused ambassador on the landing pad. The latter made an eloquent gesture, and the ranger sat down in the back seat of the car, the ambassador settled next to him, and the driver took the wheel.<br>- I never introduced myself, - the ambassador lamented. - Leonardo Palladi, ambassador of planet Earth here on Khalgalla. Oh, these soldiers, - Leonardo rolled his eyes, - young and zealous, just give them a chance to wave documents under your nose and rummage in the hold. You see, today, the seventeenth of August, we have the Annual Traditional Day of Universal Immunity, and there's no telling what goes on here!<br>- For example, pirates peacefully trading in prohibited goods right under the military's nose? - Khaynakin asked.<br>- That too, - Leonardo nodded. They had already driven out of the spaceport, and some fields of a strange lilac color were flashing by in the windows. The ranger wanted to ask what was grown there, but the ambassador spoke again. - In fact, today only ground forces may use weapons, which they do... and they also check the holds of every arriving ship for weapons, furs and drugs...<br>- What do furs have to do with it? - Faylwoker was surprised.
181	- That is considered contraband, - Leonardo declared categorically. - Just imagine how many innocent animals die daily for someone's profit! It leads to the extinction of species and a change in the ecosystem...<br>- Then why was it necessary to introduce such a "traditional day" if today anyone can engage in prohibited activity without fear of the military? - Khaynakin asked hurriedly, feeling he had touched on some personal subject.<br>- A planet that holds this event receives certain privileges from the Galaxy Council, - Palladi explained. - Tax relief, additional funds for the military fleet, social assistance... Of course, we are not insured against pirate business - our people far from always intercept illegal cargo before it reaches the buyer, but that's how it always is. And our law enforcement gets a unique opportunity! Imagine how many criminals can visit the planet with impunity on this day - we cannot detain even those who are wanted and who are not allowed to land at all. And in orbit, in most cases, warships will be waiting for them.
182	- But isn't it obvious that this is a kind of trap? - Khaynakin chuckled, watching the lilac fields give way to an industrial landscape, interspersed with some strange conifers with twisted trunks.<br>- Of course they know that, but everyone is sure he'll manage to get out of it, - Leonardo smiled. - And, sadly, not without reason - all you need is a good engine, and getting away from the military on afterburner, even with a hold packed full of prohibited goods, is no problem... But believe me, ranger, every year we detain a couple dozen criminals, they flock here like flies to honey. Some even think that a profit of a couple hundred thousand credits for the goods is worth a couple of months in prison. Especially since the conditions are decent, and our management is liberal, they might let you out earlier.<br>Suddenly the scenery outside the window froze.
183	- We're here, - Palladi announced, and was the first to get out of the car. Khaynakin climbed out after him - they stood in a huge square paved with cobblestones, glistening from recently fallen rain. The government building seemed to be in a historic district - at least, there were no ultramodern transport arteries or glass-and-concrete high-rises on the horizon. But right opposite the visitors, a pair of fountains shimmered in all the colors of the rainbow, with amazing statues in the center - representatives of all five Coalition races stood in a circle, stretching their hands up to the sun. The sculptor had managed to convey in his creation everything needed - the peleng's sly squint, the faeyan's condescending gaze, the human's open smile and the spirituality of the gaal sage... Even the huge maloq came out as if alive - the aggression on his face looked so natural that it seemed he would step down from the pedestal right now to knock the nonsense out of those who throw around overly clever words.<br>- Do you like our fountains? - Palladi asked. - They were built a couple of centuries ago... yes, about fifty years before the Klissan War began. The idea was proposed by the gaal Mikelo Aandzheloo. The sculpture seems to symbolize the unity of the races in the face of common problems... or something like that. Anyway, it turned out very symbolic, considering the war that followed.
